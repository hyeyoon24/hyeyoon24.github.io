Automated Information Extraction Pipeline for Constructing ED Knowledge Graphs from Korean Clinical Problem Lists

Hyeyoon Moon1, Eunhye Jang1, Won Chul Cha1,2

1Department of Digital Health, SAIHST, Sungkyunkwan University, Seoul, Korea ·  2Department of Emergency Medicine, Samsung Medical Center, Seoul, Korea

More standardization is not better: building knowledge graphs from Korean ED clinical notes with the right level of normalization.

TL;DR

TL;DR: We built a relation extraction pipeline for Korean ED clinical notes and compared three normalization strategies. Partial normalization (NED) beats both raw text and full SNOMED CT standardization — preserving clinical granularity matters more than maximizing standardization. Final knowledge graph: 1,983 entities, 3,208 relations from 2,000 problem lists.

Key Contributions

  • 🔹
    End-to-end pipeline for Korean ED knowledge graph construction — Few-shot prompting + schema-based validation extracts relation triplets from 2,000 heterogeneous clinical notes, yielding 1,983 entities and 3,208 relations across 4 node types and 5 relation types.
  • 🔹
    Systematic comparison of three normalization strategies — Raw Text vs Named Entity Disambiguation (NED) vs SNOMED CT Fully Specified Names (FSN), evaluated on 250 expert-annotated notes. NED achieves the best F1 (0.70), while full FSN standardization drops 5.97% by collapsing critical clinical details (TNM staging, laterality, procedural history).
  • 🔹
    General LLMs outperform reasoning-trained models for clinical RE — Llama 3.1 70B (F1 0.70) and Llama 3.3 70B (F1 0.69) beat GPT-OSS 120B (F1 0.55) by 0.15 F1, suggesting that relation extraction is fundamentally a pattern-matching task where extended chain-of-thought reasoning provides no advantage.
